HOW THE AI WORKS IN CIVILIZATION 1 AND CIVILIZATION 2
A guide to existing summaries and analyses (non-code)
Compiled October 2026

======================================================================
SHORT ANSWER
======================================================================

There's no single polished essay for either game, but there's a good
body of fan analysis. The Civ1 material is surprisingly detailed
because people have taken the game apart.


======================================================================
CIVILIZATION 1: THE BEST SOURCE
======================================================================

The most substantive work is a series of CivFanatics forum posts by a
user named "tupi". They are based on disassembling the game, so they
describe what the AI actually does rather than what players guess it
does. The posts are written in plain language with numbered rules, not
code. Topics include:

* Wonders
  The AI never builds more than one wonder per turn, needs the
  relevant advance, and can only start a wonder in a city whose
  population exceeds the wonder's shield cost divided by 100. There is
  also a simple nuclear-deterrence rule: if the human also knows
  Rocketry, the AI builds the Manhattan Project only when its military
  power is at or below the human's.
  Link: https://forums.civfanatics.com/threads/civ-1-explained-ai-wonders.656707/

* City production
  This is a set of priority rules with odd edge cases. For example,
  the AI doesn't build Carriers, and if it has no units under
  despotism it always builds Militia, even when Militia is obsolete.
  The same thread describes a per-continent "policy" that drives what
  every city on a landmass builds, and whenever that policy changes,
  the AI switches production in all its cities on that continent.
  Link: https://forums.civfanatics.com/threads/civone-request-ai-city-building-logic.648274/

* Government changes
  Link: https://forums.civfanatics.com/threads/ai-logic-revolutions-government-change.657844/

* Tax / science / luxury split
  Link: https://forums.civfanatics.com/threads/computer-player-logic-tax-science-luxuries-distribution.657037/

* How each civ's 1-7 ranking is computed (matters for things like
  civil war)
  Link: https://forums.civfanatics.com/threads/how-civilization-place-1-7-is-determined.656969/

To find more of these, search CivFanatics for "Civ 1 explained" or
browse the "decompilation" tag:
  https://forums.civfanatics.com/tags/decompilation/


======================================================================
CIVILIZATION 2: THINNER AND MORE EMPIRICAL
======================================================================

I didn't find a decompilation-based writeup comparable to tupi's.
What exists is mostly careful observation plus what the modding files
reveal:

* AI personalities
  Melth's Civ2 Let's Play on the LP Archive has a mechanics chapter on
  this (Part 30) that's a good readable overview. Its notable points
  are that the personality belongs to the civilization rather than the
  ruler, and that it found no evidence Gandhi is unusually aggressive
  in Civ2.
  Link: https://lparchive.org/Civilization-2/Update%2030

* Unit "roles" in rules.txt
  Each unit carries a role field that, according to the file's own
  comments, generally affects how computer players use the unit. This
  is the main lever behind AI unit behavior, and scenario designers
  have written a lot about it.
  Link: https://forums.civfanatics.com/threads/nukes-and-ai-response.1343

* Attitude
  Players have described permanent per-civ traits (aggressive,
  expansionist, and so on) plus a separate attitude variable that
  depends mainly on how you've treated that AI. In the Multiplayer
  Gold Edition, the AI is notoriously hostile, and patches exist to
  fix it.
  Links: https://forums.civfanatics.com/threads/ai-behaviour.397784/
         https://forums.civfanatics.com/threads/ai-attitude-change.154251

* Overall architecture
  A useful contemporary critique from the Civ3 era characterizes Civ2
  as an "every unit works on its own" approach, with no higher-level
  coordination of attacks.
  Link: https://apolyton.net/forum/miscellaneous/archives/civ3-general-archive/41443-interview-soren-johnson-on-ai?p=926777
